The Conflict

Capital One had a strong design culture, but one major initiative was moving toward a taxonomy and navigation model that risked oversimplifying how customers actually understood their financial information. The proposed structure prioritized internal categories and convenient labels over user intuition, behavioral logic, and meaningful decision support.

The risk was larger than a weak navigation system. A poorly structured experience could make complex financial data harder to understand, reduce customer confidence, and limit the product’s ability to help people act on their own information.

The Strategic Action

Rather than staying in critique mode, Van formed a self-directed, cross-functional “shadow team” to explore a better solution. The team worked outside the normal delivery path to create a functioning prototype of a gesture-based interface that translated complex financial information into a more intuitive mobile interaction model.

The work reframed the problem from “how should we categorize this information?” to “how should people move through, understand, and act on this information?” That shift changed the nature of the solution. The interface became a tool for comprehension, pattern recognition, and action.

Impact Beyond the Patent

  • Customer Win: Simplified complex financial data into clearer, more human-centered insights, contributing to a 30-day deposit increase of $10M.
  • Organizational Win: Established a “Guide/Solve” model where design intervened early, shaped the direction of the work, and helped prevent weak strategic decisions from becoming expensive product decisions.
  • Technical Win: The gesture interface, later reflected in patent US20220261083A1, expanded how the bank thought about mobile interaction and customer-facing financial tools.
  • Cultural Win: Demonstrated that designers could do more than critique finished work. They could organize talent, prototype alternatives, and create evidence for a stronger direction.
  • Strategic Win: Shifted the conversation from surface-level navigation choices to the deeper question of how digital banking could help people understand and manage financial complexity.

Strategic Takeaway

Van helped move the work from taxonomy cleanup to interaction strategy. The patent was one visible outcome, but the broader contribution was proving that design could step into ambiguity, build a better path, and turn a stalled or misdirected concept into a working model for innovation.
