Questions leaders bring to the work

How we turn a mandate into work people can own.

Where do we start when the mandate is broad?

We begin with the people, decisions, and services in scope, listening across leadership and delivery, then identify a focused question we can investigate together.

How do you make a complex service easier to understand?

We map what customers see alongside the employee work, policies, technology, and handoffs behind it, so the team can see where intent gets lost and which decisions matter next.

Who needs to be involved?

The people accountable for the direction and the people doing the work both need a voice. We bring their perspectives into the same conversation and make tradeoffs visible before they become delivery surprises.

What comes out of an initial engagement?

We agree on the question and success measures first, then use research, a service view, and a clear narrative to give the team a shared basis for the next decisions.

How do you help strategy reach execution?

We turn broad goals into blueprints, prototypes, and working rhythms that teams can test, question, and refine, staying close enough to delivery to see where the approach needs to change.

Where does AI fit in Human Agency Design?

AI can help us synthesize, explore, and support decisions, but we keep responsibility with people, examine what the system makes easier or harder, and design for informed choice.

How do you work with our existing team?

I work alongside the people who own the service, using critique and shared decision rules to build on their knowledge and leave them better equipped to carry the work forward.

How long can a partnership last?

We can begin with a focused discovery engagement, then shape an embedded phase around the work. Scope, cadence, and measures are agreed together rather than assumed in advance.

How will we know whether the work is helping?

We choose measures that fit the mandate, looking at what people understand, what teams can decide and deliver, and what changes in the service over time.

What should we bring to the first conversation?

Bring the mandate, the people it affects, and the places progress feels stuck. We can use that conversation to see whether my practice is the right fit for your next step.
