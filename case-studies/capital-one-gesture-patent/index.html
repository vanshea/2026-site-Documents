Case Study

Capital One: The Patent and Beyond

A self-directed prototype effort that turned information architecture friction into a patented gesture-based interaction model.

Capital One customers were struggling with too many screens during testing. Van assembled a small team, led prototype development, and helped turn the concept into a patented gesture-interface direction.

6 min read Updated 2026-05-02
Client Capital One
Role UX Design Lead
Year 2017
Industry Financial Services
Services
Interaction Design Information Architecture Prototyping Executive Storytelling

The Conflict

During testing, customers often mentioned that there were too many screens to find what they needed. That feedback pointed to a deeper information architecture and interaction problem: the experience required too much navigation, education, and effort for customers trying to move through financial information.

The opportunity was to create an interface that reduced that burden and made interaction feel more direct, memorable, and intuitive.

The Strategic Action

Van assembled a team to address information architecture problems identified during the redesign. As UX Design Lead, he led prototype development and technology revisions, earning second place at a hackathon.

He also created a concept video and presentation for the new technology, delivered to senior executives and the legal team. A follow-up conversation with the legal team helped develop the idea into a patent direction.

Impact Beyond the Patent

  • Customer Win: The concept focused on an interface that required minimal education and effort, directly responding to customer feedback about screen overload.
  • Technical Win: The gesture interface expanded how the team thought about interaction across devices and contexts.
  • Organizational Win: The work showed how a small team could turn observed information architecture problems into a working prototype and executive-ready story.
  • Innovation Win: The work earned hackathon recognition and continued through collaboration with the legal team to secure a patent.

Published Patent

Strategic Takeaway

Van helped turn usability friction into an innovation path. The patent was the visible artifact, but the larger contribution was recognizing a customer problem, organizing a small team around it, building a prototype, and translating the concept into a story that executives and legal partners could support.
