Case Study

NYU Curriculum Alignment

A curriculum redesign that connected information architecture to modern UX research and practice.

Van expanded NYU’s information architecture curriculum beyond classification methods into a broader human-centered UX process.

5 min read Updated 2026-05-01

The Story

Students learned traditional information architecture methods but had limited exposure to user behavior, research, and testing. They could create sitemaps and navigation systems without knowing how to validate them with people.

The Strategic Action

Van expanded the course beyond organization and classification methods to include user goals, behavior, research, usability, and real-world context.

This reframed information architecture as part of a human-centered process and connected academic training to professional UX practice.

Impact Beyond the Classroom

  • Student Win: Students learned to base design decisions on evidence rather than preference.
  • Professional Win: The course served more than 60 students each year and influenced roughly 300 designers.
  • Industry Win: Students practiced research, testing, iteration, and collaboration.
  • Strategic Win: The focus shifted from structured sitemaps to evidence-based decisions.
  • Teaching Win: The curriculum centered on what students needed to succeed in professional UX roles.

Strategic Takeaway

Van helped students move from organizing information to understanding people, giving them a stronger foundation in research and evidence-based design.
